In brief

A slow, ornament-rich prayer to Meenakshi of Madurai: “O fish-eyed one, give me bliss.” Every phrase calls out to her directly, in the vocative case. The raga's name is written into the text: vīṇā gāna daśa gamaka kriyē, “O creator of the ten gamakas in veena music”. Tradition holds that Dikshitar died on Deepavali 1835 while it was being sung, at the words mīna lōcani pāśa mōcani, “fish-eyed one, releaser from bondage”.

“mīna lōcani pāśa mōcani”
O fish-eyed one, who releases us from bondage.— Anupallavi, madhyama kāla

1Identity & Context

A temple kriti, a raga lesson and, by tradition, a composer's farewell.

ComposerMuthuswami Dikshitar (1775/76–1835), youngest of the Trinity. He wrote in Sanskrit, was a veena player and a Sri Vidya tantric, and signed his works Guruguha.
Raga · TalaGamakakriya (Dikshitar-school name for the 53rd-mela family), sung today as Purvikalyani · Adi, 2 kalai (slow)
KshetraThe Meenakshi–Sundareshvara temple, Madurai
Vibhakti8th, sambōdhana (the vocative case): every epithet is an “O…!”
MudrasRaga: daśa gamaka kriyē · Composer: vidhi guru guha vaśaṅkari
The death legend Dikshitar died at Ettayapuram on 21 October 1835, Deepavali; that much is documented. Tradition adds that he asked his disciples to sing this kriti. When they reached mīna lōcani pāśa mōcani, he said “Śivē pāhi” (“O Śivā, protect me”) and passed away. It is a cherished story rather than a documented fact, and it is the most famous story attached to any Carnatic kriti.

2Formal Structure

Slow lines, each section released by a double-speed madhyama kāla passage.

PPallavi“mīnākṣi mē mudaṃ dēhi”madhya sthayithe prayer

Two lines: mīnākṣi mē mudaṃ dēhi / mēcakāṅgi rāja mātaṅgi. The slow 2-kalai gait lets the singer unfold mīnākṣi through many sangatis.

AAnupallavi“māna mātṛ mēyē māyē”rising+ madhyama kāla

Metaphysical epithets, then the double-speed mīna lōcani pāśa mōcani / mānini kadamba vana vāsini, the line of the legend.

CCharanam“madhurā puri nilayē”imagery+ madhyama kāla

Madurai, the Pandya princess, the moon-mocking face, the veena's ten gamakas (raga mudra). Then a double-speed close: Sundaresha's beloved, Vishnu's sister, and the Guruguha mudra.

3The Raga — Gamakakriya / Purvikalyani

Two “aching” notes, R₁ and M₂, and a curl back to Pa.

Compare the ascent with its neighbours

Purvikalyani: S R₁ G₃ M₂ P D₂ P Ṡ. Low R₁, sharp M₂, then the curl D → P → Ṡ. It never climbs straight to the octave.

Gamakakriya or Purvikalyani? Both names belong to the 53rd mela, Gamanashrama. The Venkatamakhi/Dikshitar school called its form Gamakakriya, and the kriti's raga mudra uses that name. Today it is universally sung and labelled as Purvikalyani. Sources list both.
PhraseWhat to hear
G M P D P ṠThe signature curl back to Pa before upper Sa.
Ṡ N D P M GThe descent, with Ga heavily oscillated.
G M G R SR₁ leaning onto Sa: the plaintive cadence.
P D P M G M R G R SWide, slow gamakas. You cannot sing this raga as plain notes.

4Drone & Tonality

Two notes create the ache; Pa provides the resting place.

Against the tambura's S–P, R₁ (a semitone above Sa) sighs down into the tonic, and M₂ (a tritone from Sa) strains up toward Pa. The D–P curl keeps bringing the line back to Pa before it allows the octave. The effect is reverent and restrained, with longing in every cadence.

Music inside the theology Vīṇā gāna daśa gamaka kriyē names the goddess as the source of the ten gamakas, which is the raga's own name. Dikshitar, a vainika himself, writes the theory of his art into the prayer.

5Rhythm, Tala & Tempo

  • Adi tala, 2 kalai: 8 beats, each doubled, so effectively a 16-count cycle. This is Dikshitar's characteristic vilamba (slow) gait.
  • Madhyama kāla passages pack the text at double density inside that slow frame.
  • Neraval has a lot of room. Natural spots are mīna lōcani pāśa mōcani and madhurā puri nilayē. Kalpanaswaras usually follow.

6Ensemble & Texture

A standard kutcheri: voice, violin, mridangam and tambura. Dikshitar's music is idiomatically veena-like, built on heavy gamakas that are sustained and slid. That is why veena players and gamaka-rich singers are especially at home in it.

7Lyrics, Meaning & Emotional Arc

Pallavi

mīnākṣi mē mudaṃ dēhi
mēcakāṅgi rāja mātaṅgi

O Meenakshi, give me bliss! O dark-blue-bodied one, O royal Matangi!

māna mātṛ mēyē māyē
marakatacchāyē śiva jāyē
(madhyama kāla) mīna lōcani pāśa mōcani

O you who are the measure, the measurer and the measured! O Maya! O you with the glow of emerald! O wife of Shiva! O fish-eyed one, releaser from bondage! O honoured one, dweller in the Kadamba forest!

malaya dhvaja pāṇḍya rāja tanayē
vidhu viḍambana vadanē vijayē
vīṇā gāna daśa gamaka kriyē
(madhyama kāla) madhu mada mōdita hṛdayē sadayē

O dweller in Madurai, wearer of gem-studded bangles, daughter of the Pandya king Malayadhvaja! O you whose face mocks the moon, victorious one, creator of the ten gamakas in veena music! O you whose heart rejoices in the exhilaration of mead, compassionate one, beloved of Sundaresha! O sister of Vishnu (foe of Madhu and Mura), slender-waisted one, who holds Brahma and Guruguha in her sway, O Shankari!

Lalita Sahasranama echoes Māyā, pāśa mōcanī, kadamba vana vāsinī, vijayā, śātōdarī are all names in the Lalita Sahasranama. Māna mātṛ mēyē parallels its dhyāna dhyātṛ dhyēya rūpā: she is the act of knowing, the knower and the known.

The Madurai story. Meenakshi was born to the Pandya king Malayadhvaja. She ruled as a warrior queen and conquered the directions (vijayē), until she met Shiva, who came as Sundaresha. Vishnu, her brother, gave her in marriage (madhu mura ripu sōdari).

What the mapping shows The mapping shows how slowly this kriti unfolds. The first syllable mī spends a full beat-group circling Sa before nā rises, and mudaṃ dēhi ("give bliss") falls through D M G R and settles on R₁, the raga's darkest note. The legend's line, mīna lōcani pāśa mōcani, is the first place the song breaks into madhyama kāla: after long melismas, the syllables suddenly arrive one note at a time. The raga mudra daśa gamaka kriyē sits on moving pairs (G M · R G · G N · D M) that call for exactly the gamakas it names. At the end, the composer mudra guru guha races down S sn N D, back toward the pallavi.

9Significance

  • The concert benchmark for Purvikalyani, sung as a main or sub-main piece.
  • A defining legend: the composer's death during the song makes it one of the most emotionally charged works in the repertoire.
  • Dikshitar's method in miniature: raga mudra, composer mudra, Sri Vidya epithets and a temple's iconography in one short text.

10Performance Practice & Recordings

The main interpretive choices are the tempo of the 2-kalai cycle, how much room to give the madhyama kāla passages, and where to place neraval.

11Listening Guide

A typical ~20-minute concert rendition. Times are approximate.

  • 0:00Alapana Alapana — R₁ sighing onto Sa; the M₂ ache; D-P-Ṡ curls; long gamakas on Ga.
  • 6:00Pallavi P — the slow 2-kalai gait; sangatis on mīnākṣi.
  • 8:30Anupallavi A — māna mātṛ mēyē rises.
  • 9:30“mīna lōcani pāśa mōcani” legend line A — the double-speed surge.
  • 10:00Charanam C — Madurai imagery; catch daśa gamaka kriyē, where the raga names itself.
  • 12:30Madhyama kāla C — madhu mada mōdita… ending on guru guha.
  • 13:00Neraval + kalpanaswaras development Impro — often on mīna lōcani or madhurā puri.
  • 19:00Return to pallavi Close — quiet devotion.
First listen vs re-listen On a first listen, feel the contrast between the slow lines and the double-speed madhyama kāla. On a re-listen, follow R₁ and M₂, the two aching notes, and catch the moment the raga's name is sung.

12Must-Listen

Anupallavi → “mīna lōcani pāśa mōcani”

≈ 3 min

Why this one. The raga's most characteristic phrases, the kriti's most striking change of pace, and the line tradition ties to Dikshitar's last breath.

Recommended. M.S. Subbulakshmi, then D.K. Pattammal for the austere Dikshitar-school reading.
