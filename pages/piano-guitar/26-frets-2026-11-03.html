    Warm-up wakes the fingers. Technique is the week's new skill. Chords & rhythm drills it with a metronome. Song time puts it to use in real music, so practice never feels like homework.

    Five rules that make 15 minutes work

    1. Same time, same place. Keep the Taylor on a stand where you can see it. Fewer steps to start means more days played.
    2. Three clean reps, then +4 bpm. If you miss twice, drop 8 bpm. The tempo ladder in each week shows where you should be.
    3. Metronome for every drill. Your sense of time gets better only if something checks it.
    4. Record every Saturday. Listening back shows you things you can't hear while you play.
    5. Stop at 15. Extra time goes to playing songs for fun, not to longer drills. Wanting more tomorrow is the goal.

    Field guide

    Setup, technique pictures, and fixes for the problems everyone runs into.

    Two guitars, two jobs

    • Taylor (acoustic): strumming, fingerpicking, singing along, film songs. Keep a capo in the case. Heavier strings build finger strength quickly.
    • Epiphone SG (electric): riffs, power chords, blues, bends and lead. Lighter strings and lower action make barre chords much easier, so learn each barre on the SG first, then move it to the Taylor.
    • Amp starting points: Clean: gain 2, bass 5, mid 6, treble 6. Crunch for riffs: gain 6, mid 7. Neck pickup for warm lead, bridge pickup for bite.
    • Practise SG chord work unplugged or clean at first. Distortion hides mistakes.

    Setup checklist

    • A tuner (clip-on or a phone app). Tune at the start of every session.
    • New strings if yours are over 3 months old. Try 10s on the SG and light 12s on the Taylor.
    • Picks: 0.60–0.73 mm for strumming, a firmer 0.88 mm for riffs and lead.
    • A phone stand for Saturday recordings.
    • The guitar on a stand, not in its case. This one change adds more practice days than anything else.
    • Sore fingertips are normal for the first 2–3 weeks on the acoustic. Sharp pain or numbness means stop for the day.

    Where to put your fingertip

    Reading tab in 20 seconds

    Buzzing

    Move the fingertip closer to the fret, arch the finger more, and check the thumb sits behind the neck rather than wrapping over it.

    Muted strings

    A neighbouring finger is touching the string. Bring the wrist forward slightly so fingertips come down vertically.

    Changes too slow

    Practise only the change, 1 minute, counting clean ones. Lift fingers together as one shape, and use anchor fingers where they exist.

    Strumming hand stops

    Keep the hand moving down-up all the time. Silent slots are strums that miss the strings, not pauses.

    Dead barre strings

    Roll the index onto its bony edge, keep it hugging the fret, and pull back gently with the arm instead of squeezing with the thumb.

    Rushing

    Count out loud with the metronome for one minute. Then drop the click to beats 2 and 4 only.

    Losing your place in a song

    Write the chord map on one line per section, for example V: G Em C D ×2. Practise the joins between sections.

    Bored or stuck

    Repeat the week with an alternate song from the library. A plateau usually breaks 3–5 days after you stop expecting it to.

    After week 26: where next

    • CAGED system and triads: see the whole neck as five connected chord shapes.
    • Deeper fingerstyle: Dust in the Wind, Blackbird, or film-song instrumentals.
    • Blues lead: the B.B. King box, the major/minor pentatonic mix, call-and-response with your own recordings.
    • Raga on guitar: slides and bends as gamakas, drone-based alapana over the C drone loop, playing Carnatic varnams on single strings.
    • Songwriting: use the four progressions from the jam loops and write a melody over one of them.
